About this project

CitationAI is a small, free, open-source tool that lets a scholar turn an AI conversation into a proper citation. You paste (or link to) the exchange, fill in who was involved and when, and choose either a standard academic style — APA, MLA, Chicago, Harvard, Vancouver, or IEEE — or your own custom set of fields; the tool then produces both a reference-list entry and an in-text citation, ready to copy into a manuscript. Everything runs in your browser: nothing is sent anywhere except the link-hosting step you choose yourself, and the whole project is open source so anyone can inspect it, self-host it, or improve it.

There is no established mechanism in scholarly work for citing an AI agent in a way that preserves a record of how it was produced. There is no single, universally adopted citation standard that fully preserves an AI agent's data — including identity, configuration, inputs, chain of thought, and other outputs — so that another researcher can reconstruct exactly how the reference was produced. At a moment when agents are increasingly contributing to published scholarly work, there is no established standard for citing that contribution.

CitationAI is a beta prototype, designed to validate the concept and to serve as a vehicle for soliciting feedback about emerging best practices around agentic collaboration in scholarly work. More generally, CitationAI aspires to catalyze conversations about how academics should employ agents in their teaching and research.

Conventional citation styles — MLA, APA, Harvard, Chicago — situate a source reference in the text and let a reader trace it back to its origin. AI conversations have no equivalent: no standard record, no durable link. Whether the output is code, natural language, or some other type of artifact, there should be an easy, user-friendly way to tag those contributions with a citation that references either agentic or human authorship.

This experimental pilot, built with AI, is meant to solicit feedback about how AI should be used in research and teaching. Some of the questions that guide this investigation:

  1. How might such an AI citation style be adopted by leading journals and conferences?
  2. What records are helpful to retain? How can a system be designed that allows for easy, even automatic, referencing? What features should CitationAI have?
  3. How can it be designed to be open and free to all users? There is an open question in this iteration of whether to host the data, or to provide an affordance that links to the chain in the chatbot that produced it — and there may be other, better options to consider.
  4. Who owns the data — the user, or the AI company? Is it fair use? There are open questions about intellectual property, as well as other legal considerations.
Nothing you paste here is sent anywhere except to the link or hosting step you choose yourself; the fingerprint and file are generated in your browser. CitationAI is free and open source under the MIT license — read the code, self-host it, or send improvements on GitHub.
