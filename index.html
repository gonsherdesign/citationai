CitationAI

CitationAI is a free, open-source scholarly tool for creating citations for AI agents. Share the conversation, either automatically by “sharing” or pasting the text in the appropriate field. Then select the citation style. CitationAI will prepare the appropriate reference and in-text citation with a link to the chain of thought that is cited as a reference.

About this project

There are no universal mechanism in scholarly work for citing an AI agent in a way that preserves a full record of how it was produced. There are no established conventions, yet, for citations styles that fully preserves an AI agent's data, including identity, configuration, inputs, chain of thought, and other outputs. This is necessary in order to allow peer researchers the ability to reconstruct exactly how the reference was produced. At a moment when agents are increasingly contributing to published scholarly work, there is no established standard for citing that contribution. [Claude,1, 2026]

CitationAI is a beta prototype, designed to validate the concept and to serve as a vehicle for soliciting feedback about emerging best practices around agentic collaboration in scholarly work. More generally, CitationAI aspires to catalyze conversations about how academics should employ agents in their teaching and research.

Conventional citation styles — MLA, APA, Harvard, Chicago, IEEE — situate the reference within the text, allowing the reader to trace the idea back to its origin. AI conversations have no equivalent; no standard record, no durable link. Whether the output is code, natural language, or some other type of artifact, there should be an easy, user-friendly way to tag those contributions with a citation that references either agentic or human authorship.

This experimental pilot, built with Claude AI, and as design research project for the Creative Scholars Project, is meant to be a discursive artifact; a way to solicit feedback, and reflection, about how AI should be used in research and teaching. Some of the questions that guide this investigation:

  1. How might such an AI citation style be adopted by leading journals and conferences?
  2. What records are helpful to retain? How can a system be designed that allows for easy, even automatic, referencing? What features should CitationAI have?
  3. How can it be designed to be open and free to all users? There is an open question in this iteration of whether to host the data, or to provide an affordance that links to the chain in the chatbot that produced it — and there may be other, better options to consider.
  4. Who owns the data — the user, or the AI company? Is it fair use? There are open questions about intellectual property, as well as other legal considerations.
  5. What other considerations should we be considering in the development of future iterations of this project? How should we make use of agents in scholarly work, if at all?
